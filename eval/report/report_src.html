model-detection · phase A

Brand and Person Detector Sweep

Eight detectors measured against hand-drawn box ground truth, under a schema where brand means the mark itself and person is one word. Boxes replace frame presence as the headline and change the ranking. The finding that drives every decision here is that brand detection is a small-object problem: most real marks sit under 24 pixels, and what a model can do about that turns out to depend on its architecture.

FINDINGSWhat the boxes changed

Frame presence could not rank these detectors. Box ground truth can, and four of its results change what should be built rather than merely who wins.

Brand detection is a small-object problem, and that is the binding constraint

Half the ground-truth marks are under 24 px on their short side and two thirds are under 32 px. Every detector's recall falls off a cliff there: Grounding DINO covers 0.92 of marks 80 px and larger but only 0.41 below 16 px, and it is the best of the field at both ends.

This is not a prompt problem and no amount of prompt engineering will move it. It is resolution, and it had a concrete consequence for Phase B: a min_crop_pixels of 32 discards two thirds of the marks the detector is being asked to find. That parameter is now measured rather than argued and sits at 16, which keeps 81% — but the detector still has to find a 22 px mark before any of it applies.

Only two backends can find brand marks at all

OWLv2 and Grounding DINO reach brand AP 0.310 and 0.166. The next best is 0.062, and the YOLOE and YOLO-World text heads sit between 0.02 and 0.06 — an order of magnitude down, not a close third. Bootstrapped over frames, both leaders clear the rest of the field with no overlap; they do not cleanly separate from each other, so treat this as a two-model shortlist rather than a winner.

The split is architectural, not a matter of tuning, and it is the same split the bare-word ablation found: MobileCLIP-style encoders compare one pooled prompt vector against region vectors, and a mark is not a category they have a vector for. Both models that work have a BERT text encoder cross-attending to image features.

Person is solved, and the cheapest model wins it

Plain YOLO11 takes person on every measure that matters — AP 0.743, mean IoU 0.89, 53 ms per frame — against 0.634 for OWLv2 at 3.6× the cost. A closed COCO-80 detector beats every open-vocabulary model at the one class COCO was built around.

So the two classes want different detectors, and nothing forces one model to serve both.

Adding symbol to the brand prompts makes things worse

Tested head-to-head: it costs the leader (OWLv2 brand AP 0.310 → 0.284) and gains nothing anywhere else. It is not junk — 8 of its 14 detections on ground-truth frames land on real marks — but it wins the argmax on boxes logo already had, and re-ranks them lower. Rejected; the list stays at six terms.

The detectors find the marks a person notices, and miss the rest

Ground-truth marks have a median short side of 22 px. The marks OWLv2 and Grounding DINO actually return have a median of 37 and 40 px. They are not boxing loosely — mean IoU is 0.78 and 0.82 — they are finding a larger-than-average subset and leaving the small half on the floor.

For the tagger this is arguably the right failure. A 14 px logo on a distant LED hoarding will not embed to anything a logo pool can retrieve, so missing it costs little. What it does mean is that brand recall against exhaustive ground truth understates usable performance, and the report says so rather than quoting 0.44 as if every missed mark were a loss.

RESULT 2 — DE-DUPLICATIONSix terms, one mark

Asking for six near-synonymous brand terms means a mark answering to several of them comes back several times. Grounding DINO emits 768 overlapping brand-box pairs from 1,245 detections on the ground-truth frames, 399 of them between different terms. Every repeat is a false positive here and a wasted SigLIP forward pass in production.

Class-agnostic suppression buys precision for nothing

Grounding DINO's brand AP goes 0.166 → 0.205 and its precision 0.25 → 0.31, with class-agnostic coverage unchanged at 0.61 — the gain is entirely removed duplicates, not newly found marks. Person AP rises for every backend. It also cuts the crops the pipeline would embed by about a third.

Suppression must run across prompt terms, not within each: per-class NMS would keep the logo box and the letter logo box of one wordmark, and those cross-term pairs are the majority of the problem. It stays inside a class bucket though — a person box overlapping a logo box is two real findings, the mark and the player wearing it.

The threshold is set by a constraint, because fitting AP picks a harmful value

Brand AP peaks at IoU 0.45. That value is wrong: coverage falls there (gdino 0.609 → 0.594, owlv2 0.542 → 0.516), meaning suppression is merging marks that are genuinely distinct — adjacent logos on one hoarding, a wordmark inside an emblem. AP cannot see it because both boxes were being counted anyway.

So the rule is coverage must not fall, and the threshold is the smallest value satisfying it: 0.6. It also happens to be where person AP peaks.

The YOLOE text heads do not generalise their threshold, and presence never showed it

yoloe26-text's person F1 looks competitive in-sample at 0.656 against yolo11's 0.734. Held out it collapses to 0.483 while yolo11 barely moves to 0.719 — and the eleven chosen thresholds span 0.043–0.171, a four-fold range, against yolo11's 0.101–0.119.

A four-fold spread means there is no single config value that works across content types. That is a deployment property no aggregate metric in this report would have surfaced, and it is the strongest argument for YOLO11 on person: not that it scores highest, but that its score is the one that survives contact with unseen footage.

Grounding DINO is the best-calibrated brand detector

It loses the least to held-out evaluation of any brand model (−0.023) and its threshold range is tight at 0.142–0.162. OWLv2 still leads on held-out F1 (0.339 against 0.286) but gives up more (−0.058), and the two remain within each other's confidence intervals.

Targeting precision instead of F1 tightens both further: at a 0.6 precision target, OWLv2 holds 0.320 held-out F1 with a −0.012 drop and gdino 0.120 with −0.018. If a wrong brand tag is worse than a missing one, that is the mode to ship.

Grounding DINO and OWLv2 cannot be scaled by resolution at all

Grounding DINO collapses to AP 0.001 — and takes person down with it, 0.627 → 0.021. Person collapsing is the tell, because resolution does not destroy person detection, so this was checked as a plumbing bug before being believed.

It is not one. Running --hf-imgsz 800 against the stock default returns byte-identical output, so the override is faithful; and batch 1 against batch 4 gives identical detections and identical box geometry, so it is not batch padding interacting with target_sizes. The failure is visible in the boxes: median normalised box width goes 0.038 → 0.487 → 0.623 while the detection count falls 2589 → 365 → 240. The model stops localising and emits a few large diffuse boxes.

That is DETR-family behaviour. The decoder's learned reference points and object queries are tuned to the training resolution of 800 shortest edge by 1333 longest, and a larger input moves them off the distribution they were fitted to. OWLv2 is the same story with a softer curve: it needs interpolate_pos_encoding=True to accept any non-960 input, and gains nothing when it does.

At the shipping operating point the gap is far smaller than AP suggests

Re-running the held-out threshold selection at 1280 puts yoloe26-text's brand F1 at 0.267 against Grounding DINO's 0.286 — seven percent apart, where AP is 1.5× apart. Its threshold is also the most stable in the study: 0.007 across all eleven folds, with a held-out drop of 0.004.

AP grades the whole score-ordered list; held-out F1 grades the operating point you actually ship. They disagree here because yoloe26 emits few brand detections and nearly all of them are kept, so its ranking has little room to be wrong. Coverage remains the honest gap — 0.25 against 0.61 — and coverage is what says how many distinct marks ever reach the index.

RESULT 5 — THE GROUND TRUTH ITSELFWhat was labelled, and how

The ground truth is the one artefact with nothing downstream to catch its mistakes: a mislabelled frame does not announce itself, it just moves every detector's AP in the same direction. So it is shown here rather than asserted.

Seeding did not decide the answer

Boxes were pre-drawn from a balanced union of Grounding DINO, OWLv2, yoloe11-text and YOLO11, so labelling was accept-and-nudge rather than draw-from-scratch. That saves a great deal of time and introduces an obvious risk: ground truth that is really just a consensus of the models being graded, which would flatter all four and penalise nothing.

It did not happen, and the labelling record shows why. Of 524 proposals, 225 were rejected and 158 boxes were drawn fresh where no proposal came within IoU 0.5. For brand the independence is near-total: 139 of 192 marks (72%) were drawn by hand, because the seed detectors simply had not found them. Person is the opposite — 222 of 254 accepted verbatim — which is consistent with person being the class the field already solves.

The one caveat worth stating. The 53 brand boxes that did come from seeds are, by construction, boxes some detector drew, so those four backends get an IoU advantage on that subset. It is bounded by the 72% that did not, and it cannot explain a ranking gap of 0.310 against 0.022.

Presence and boxes agree on the top, and disagree everywhere else

Both put OWLv2 and Grounding DINO first on brand, which is reassuring. Below that they diverge completely: presence separates yoloe11-text (0.82) from yoloe26-pf (0.06) by 13×, while boxes separate them by 2× — and presence rates yoloe26-text and yoloe11-text as near-equals of the leaders at 0.82 against 0.91, where boxes put them five times apart.

A detector firing somewhere on a frame that contains a logo is a low bar when 62 of 100 frames contain one. Boxes ask whether it found the mark.

Presence labels were corrected from the boxes

The 100-frame presence labels were made under the old 8-class schema, with brand derived from its logo class. Where box ground truth exists it answers the same question exactly, so it overrides — and it disagreed on four frames, all in the same direction: the old pass asked whether a frame had a prominent logo, the box pass caught every mark including small ones. Those four had been scored as false positives against every detector that correctly found the mark. Brand positives went 58 → 62, and OWLv2's fp@.9 improved 0.21 → 0.16.

Person needed no correction at all: 25 of 25 exact. The remaining 75 frames still carry derived labels, so the two halves of that table are not labelled to the same standard. Which is the argument for box AP as the headline, not an argument for patching presence further.

Object nouns overshadow the mark, and the crop moves with them

Asked for sportswear, a detector returns the hoodie. Asked for logo, it returns the wordmark on it. Under the 101-noun list only 1% of Grounding DINO's brand-bucket crops were mark-like — 63% were shoes — and 51% were under 32 px of something that was not a mark. Under the six mark terms, 100% are mark-like.

Surfaces reproduce the same failure one level up: adding banner and billboard moved the box from the logo to the hoarding carrying it. The rule that came out of this is the schema's whole content — name the mark, never the thing it sits on.

Bare abstract words split the field by text-encoder architecture

Prompted with the single word brand, Grounding DINO grounds it to real marks and OWLv2 partially does. The YOLOE and YOLO-World heads return essentially nothing — one detection in 100 frames for YOLO-World.

The cause is structural. MobileCLIP-style encoders pool a prompt into one vector and compare it against region vectors, and there is no region that looks like the concept brand. Grounding DINO's BERT encoder cross-attends to image features and can ground an abstract word onto concrete evidence. This is the same divide that decides Result 1, seen from a different angle.

RESULT 9 — IMAGE PROMPTINGSettled, and it simplifies the design

42 single-exemplar runs, one reference image at a time rather than an averaged batch, using the logo pool for brand and new person_* exemplars for person. The conclusion holds after the reframing and removes a whole branch from the pipeline.

Brand visual prompting is degenerate, and its recall number is an artifact

Prompted with a Nike or UPS logo crop, YOLOE returns near-full-frame boxes. It scores 0.44 recall on frame presence, which looks like a result until the sheet shows there is no localisation happening — a box covering the frame contains the logo trivially. The metric counts it; the crop is worthless to an embedder.

Person visual prompting works and is redundant

Per-exemplar person prompts give tight, correct boxes. They also give nothing the word person did not already give, and identification — which person, which pose — is a downstream query against the embeddings regardless.

So the target parameter can be text-only, image similarity stays a query-side operation on the vector index, and the detector choice is freed from YOLOE and its AGPL-3.0 licence. That last point is not a small one.

An argument in the design notes did not survive its own test

The README argued NaFlex must beat the fixed-384 variant because a square resize squashes wide wordmarks, and that wordmarks are this index's payload. The wide column tests exactly that — aspect ratio ≥ 2.5, which is half the pool (median 2.59, p90 5.57, max 10.4), so the test has teeth.

It did not hold. base-naflex led at 400 brands (0.937 against 0.932) and large-384 led at 1,500 (0.943 against 0.947). The direction flipped, so it is noise. The squash is a real mechanism, but it does not register at retrieval level. NaFlex still wins — on cost, which is measured.

Cross-domain costs a third of the recall, and the pool bounds the rest

Same-domain 0.93 against cross-domain 0.60. The powered number is the optimistic one; a realistic expectation for the shipped pipeline is nearer 0.6. Only 15 queries were possible — the footage is NBA and NFL broadcast, and its marks are mostly leagues, teams and US insurers that the 2,960-brand pool does not contain.

That deserves more attention than the model comparison that surfaced it.

Below ~10 px a crop does not fail quietly — it fails confidently

At 8 px the hit/miss gap is negative: wrong answers come back with a higher cosine than right ones. No downstream similarity threshold can filter that. Those crops are not weak evidence, they are noise asserting itself, and they would poison a similarity-gated index rather than merely miss.

That is the argument for having a floor at all, and it is the half of the original reasoning that survived. The gap recovers monotonically with size and is back to native by about 32 px.

The trade turns over at 16

Marginal precision bought per unit of recall given up: 1.9 from 8→12, 0.75 from 12→16, then 0.39 from 16→20 and 0.15 from 24→32. Below 16 the gate buys more than it costs; above it, less. At 32 it was discarding two thirds of the marks to buy a precision point already within 0.10 of reach.

Throughput cost of the change is 8–11%, not the 2.3× the mark distribution implies — the detectors do not find most sub-32px marks in the first place. Crops per frame after max_detections: yoloe-26 17.4→18.9, grounding-dino 14.5→15.6, yolo11 10.9→12.1. Person is barely affected anywhere in this range; in practice this is a brand parameter.

The deciding argument is which mistake can be undone

A floor set too low is recoverable: every tag carries additional_info.upscale, which at a fixed patch budget is a monotone function of crop area, so a consumer wanting a stricter floor filters for it. A floor set too high is not — the crop was never embedded, and getting it back means re-decoding the video and re-running detection, at 98–293 ms per frame against ~6 ms per crop to embed. Given a trade this close, take the side whose mistake is reversible downstream.

The retrieval curve was measured on clean reference art downscaled cleanly. A real broadcast crop of the same pixel size carries motion blur and compression this does not, so every recall figure above is optimistic and 16 is a floor rather than a comfortable operating point. Whether real degradation pushes the answer to 20 is inside that caveat and is not settled here.

DEPLOYMENTWhat each choice costs to run

The two classes want different detectors, so the tagger runs two — plus one embedder. Detector cost scales with frames; embedder cost scales with crops, which is frames times detections per frame, so a cheap detector that emits many boxes can cost more downstream than an expensive one that emits few.

SigLIP 2 NaFlex measured at 5.5 ms per crop (182 crops/s, batch 32) and is flat across crop sizes: it pads to a fixed token budget, so a 24 px wordmark costs the same as a 300 px person. Small marks are not cheap to embed.

The 2× buys 2.4× the marks, and little else

At each model's best resolution Grounding DINO costs 2× yoloe26-text end to end. For that it returns brand coverage 0.61 against 0.25 and box AP 0.205 against 0.133 — but held-out F1 of only 0.286 against 0.267. The choice is therefore about how many distinct marks reach the index, not about whether the ones that do are right.

The decision therefore turns on sampling rate rather than on the models. Brand marks persist for seconds, so fps 1–2 is the right rate for brand and at that rate Grounding DINO costs 52 minutes to 1h44 for a two-hour video — acceptable for offline tagging. It is fps 8 that makes it untenable at 6h56, and fps 8 is not what brand needs. Person can run at a higher rate independently, since YOLO11 is cheap.

Container image size

Adding a second detector does not change it. The image is 14.5 GB and carries no weights at all — they download at first load into the mounted cache under /root/.cache. What dominates is the dependency layer at 12.2 GB: bundled CUDA libraries 4.1 GB, torch 1.7 GB, triton 542 MB, then ffmpeg and build-essential at 915 MB.

Both candidate stacks are already installed. Grounding DINO is transformers, which SigLIP 2 requires anyway; YOLOE and YOLO11 are both ultralytics. So either choice leaves the image at 14.5 GB and changes only the runtime cache.

ANSWERSThe two methods, and what this measures

Are there two routes to brand and person — targeted detection from prompts, or general detection filtered downstream by vector similarity?

yes, and they are now measured separately

They are the class-aware and class-agnostic columns of Result 1. Targeted detection is AP: the detector was told what to look for and its label is checked. General detection filtered downstream is cov: did a crop land on the object, whatever it was called.

For person, both work and the general route wins on cost — YOLO11 covers 0.96 of people at 53 ms with no prompting at all. For brand, the general route is not available: the best prompt-free coverage is 0.12 and YOLO11's is 0.04. Nothing in a general detector's vocabulary is a mark, so there is no set of general detections to filter down to one. Brand requires targeted detection, and specifically requires one of the two cross-attention models.

Is the pipeline right — source media, optional text target, detect, embed, and identification is downstream?

yes, with one qualification

The measurements support it. Identification is cosine similarity against a pool, so the detector owes only crops that are tight around the entity — which is what mean IoU measures, and it is 0.78–0.89 across the usable models.

The qualification is that "more general but still tight" has a floor. Brand marks below about 24 px are found less than half the time by the best model available, so the crop the embedder needs sometimes does not exist. That is a resolution budget question, not a prompt question, and it is the first thing Phase B should quantify.

Is one word enough for person, and mark-only enough for brand?

both confirmed

person alone gives coverage of 0.92–0.97 on four backends; no concrete-noun list improved on it. Mark-only took brand crops from 1% mark-like to 100%, and the symbol test says the list is now at the point where adding terms costs more than it returns.

NEXTWhat is left

Phases A and B are both closed: the detectors, the embedder and every crop parameter now have a measured value behind them. What remains is ground truth and one product question, not modelling.

Pool freshness is a product question now, not a modelling one

KIA scores 0.00 for both embedders because the pool holds the old oval logo and the footage shows the 2021 rebrand wordmark. Nothing in the model stack fixes that — a correct encoder faithfully reports that two different marks are different. The index is only as current as its references, so what is needed is an owner and a refresh cadence for the pool: how a rebrand gets noticed, who adds the new art, whether old art is retired or kept as an alias for archive footage. Those are decisions about a data asset, and no experiment here can settle them.

It also bounds what the measured numbers mean. Only 5 of the brands in this footage exist in the pool at all; the broadcast marks that dominate it — State Farm, ESPN, NBC, team logos — are simply absent. Coverage of the pool, not the choice of encoder, is the ceiling on end-to-end identification.

Tiled or higher-resolution inference for small marks

Still the highest-value modelling question, since small objects are the binding constraint. min_crop_pixels is now measured and no longer part of it: at 16 the gate keeps 81% of marks, so the loss has moved back upstream to what the detector finds. Coverage below 16 px is 0.41 even for the best model in the field, and tiling is the standard remedy — it costs throughput and has not been tried.

Extend the box ground truth

25 frames is enough to separate the field but not to rank its top two. Bootstrapping AP over frames (400 resamples) puts OWLv2 at 0.310 [0.219, 0.441] and Grounding DINO at 0.166 [0.112, 0.246] — intervals that touch. Both are cleanly clear of yoloe26-text at 0.062 [0.027, 0.109], so which architecture is settled and which of the two models is not.

The other gap is coverage of the footage itself. The 11 clips are heavily sports-weighted, and the mark-size distribution driving every conclusion here comes mostly from NBA crowd frames. Another 25 frames from non-sports sources would test whether the small-mark floor is a property of brands or of basketball.

More negative frames

Two of 25 frames have no brand and three of 100 have no person. False-positive rates are therefore estimated from very few frames, and person's cannot be estimated at all. Frames with no people and no marks are cheap to add and would make precision mean something.

Done · the derived presence labels are retired

Frame presence is no longer scored by default. score_bp.py now prints cost and breadth — throughput, latency, detection counts, off-prompt tag counts — none of which need ground truth, and the presence table is behind --presence with its provenance stated. Ranking is box_gt/score_boxes.py; thresholds are box_gt/operating_point.py.

The 75 frames were not relabelled, because relabelling them would have meant inventing ground truth rather than measuring it. If the 100-frame set is wanted back as an instrument, those frames need a human pass under the mark definition — but nothing currently depends on it.

METHODHow the numbers were produced

Runs

100 frames frozen from 11 clips. Every backend runs ungated — confidence near zero, high detection cap — and each detection's score is written out, because detector scores are not comparable across models and fixing one threshold for all of them would rank them by score inflation. The scorer sweeps each detector over its own threshold grid.

Throughput is measured batched, which is how a tagger consumes video; latency is measured at batch size 1 with the CUDA queue synchronised, which is what a single-image request pays. Warm-up frames are excluded from both.

Box matching

Greedy by descending score: each detection takes the highest-IoU unclaimed ground-truth box of the same class above IoU 0.5. One ground-truth box matches once, so duplicate detections on the same object count as false positives. AP is 101-point interpolated, COCO convention. A detection whose centre falls in an ignore region is dropped entirely — neither true nor false positive — because dense crowds cannot be boxed exhaustively and punishing a detector for finding a real person nobody drew would measure the labeller.

Precision is deliberately not reported class-agnostically. A prompt-free run that boxes a chair is not wrong, it is just not boxing a target, and counting it as a false positive would measure the schema rather than the detector.

The Grounding DINO repair

The first sweep recorded Grounding DINO at F1 0.27 with 94% of detections carrying an empty label, and blamed prompt batching. That was wrong on both counts. Its post-processor keeps boxes above threshold but decodes labels above text_threshold, which defaulted to 0.25 while boxes were kept at 0.05 — so every box between the two kept its geometry and lost its label. Chunking prompts could never have fixed it.

Labels are also always decoded from a BERT token span, which produced wordpiece fragments like ##board and spans straddling two phrases. Both are fixed: the thresholds are bound together, and attribution is computed from the fast tokenizer's character offsets — each phrase's span mapped to token positions, each box assigned the phrase carrying its highest probability. One clean phrase per box. Empty labels went to 0% and the model got 3.2× faster as a side effect of not decoding spans it would discard.

No tag map

Synonym bridging from the prompt-free vocabularies onto brand/person was previously done with a hand-built keyword map. It was retired: built from keyword families with veto lists, it could not survive compounds — an activity veto swallowed fishing boat and wedding cake, a person rule captured record player — and one review pass found around ninety such errors across 4,607 terms, with each correction moving the errors rather than removing them.

It also turned out to be unnecessary. Under the mark schema there are no synonyms left to bridge: logo and person appear verbatim in every vocabulary involved, so all eight backends score directly on their own labels. Class-agnostic coverage answers the question the map was there for, and answers it by measurement.

Phase B protocols

The embedder comparison and the crop-size sweep both use the 2,960-brand logo pool, because it carries real brand identity and the box ground truth does not — it labels brand, not which brand. One image per brand is the query, the rest go in the gallery, so difficulty scales with the gallery rather than with the brand count.

An earlier design built pairs from the box ground truth by matching boxes across frames of one clip at IoU ≥ 0.3. It was discarded as invalid: frames are sampled seconds to a minute apart, so in crowd footage a box at the same position is a different person. It scored person recall@1 at 0.047, produced only 24 brand pairs, and returned identical numbers for both candidates — the signature of a metric measuring its own construction.

The crop-size curve downscales pool queries and retrieves against native-resolution references. Clean art downscaled cleanly is easier than a real broadcast crop of the same pixel size, so that curve is optimistic and is read as a floor rather than an operating point.

Reproducing

Everything lives in eval/: frameset/ the frozen frames and labels, tools/ the scripts, experiments/01–09 one directory per experiment, box_gt/ the ground truth with its task builder, browser labelling tool and scorer, report/ this page. The labelling tool is a single self-contained HTML file with all 25 frames embedded.

Phase B specifically: tools/compare_embedders.py (pool retrieval), tools/crop_to_pool.py (detected crop against the pool), tools/size_sensitivity.py (min_crop_pixels), tools/padding_sensitivity.py (crop_padding), tools/bench_embed.py (GPU-only throughput). That the shipped tagger reproduces these vectors is itself checked, by tools/verify_embedder_wiring.py — it confirms the crop is the padded box while the reported box is un-padded, and that the tagger's vision-tower call matches the benchmark's get_image_features call to 2.4e−07 in fp32.
